On w3runn3rs, you get one club. Just one.

That might sound limiting. It's actually the most powerful thing about the platform.

When every kilometer you run counts exclusively for your club — not split across three clubs you half-heartedly belong to — the choice of *which* club to join becomes significant. It's a commitment. And commitments change behavior.

## What to Look For in a Running Club

### 1. Training Style Match

Are you a 5K pace runner, a marathon grinder or a trail beast? Not all clubs train the same way. The best clubs have a clear identity — and their training reflects it.

Ask yourself: do I want to *race* or do I want to *socialize*? The best clubs do both, but they'll usually lean one way.

### 2. Community Vibe

The runs end. The community doesn't.

Pay attention to how club members interact outside of training. Do they travel to races together? Do they celebrate each other's PRs? Is there a culture of lifting each other up?

A great running club feels like a team even when no one is running.

### 3. Geographic Presence

Some clubs are local. Some are global. Some have chapters in every city.

If you travel for races — and if you're reading this, you probably do — a club with a global footprint means you'll have training partners anywhere you go.

### 4. Competitive Drive

Leaderboards matter. W3runn3rs is built around them.

If you want competition to be real, join a club that *wants to win*. Clubs that push each other, track their kilometers and show up week after week are the ones that make the leaderboards interesting.

## The One-Club Rule Changes Everything

When you can only belong to one club, you choose carefully. You commit fully. And that commitment creates the kind of loyalty that makes running clubs worth building in the first place.

Your effort isn't diluted. Your kilometers aren't shared. Your wins belong to your crew.
